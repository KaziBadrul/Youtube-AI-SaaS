# S3 — Worker interruption and recovery

**Verdict: PASS — fake-provider crash/recovery and real local child supervision.** [Raw evidence](S3.json).

**Claim:** persisted attempt identity, durable recovered bytes, fencing and confirmation of external/child cessation can prevent unsafe overlapping production and retain spending liability.

**Failure sought:** blind duplicate billable requests after a crash, dropped unknown reservations, stale/current replacement, cancellation losing usable work, or a lease permitting new work while an old child still executes.

**Minimum experiment / pass criteria:** [predeclared S3 plan](PLAN.md#s3). Three repeats at each of ten worker boundaries: prepared, before submission, submission started, response received, staged, validated, installed, metadata registered, financial registered, selected. Separate fake provider DB deliberately permits duplicate submission; tests assert actual call counts. No duplication, uncertainty retains holds/blocks claims, received output reused, canceled/stale results history-only, replayed settlement once, unaffected work preserved. Lease expiry alone must not unlock/replace a surviving child.

**Command:** `/tmp/alpha-feasibility-venv/bin/python spikes/local-feasibility/s3.py`. Requires process-inspection/signal access for its own temporary groups; no provider permissions.

**Observed:** 30 crash cases passed. Prepared/possible submission remains conservatively uncertain without independent outcome proof; held amount/allowance stay reserved and another queued job cannot claim the lane. Completed receipts/staged bytes are installed and registered without another fake submission. Delivery replay produces one ledger event. Five fake behaviors cover success, definite paid failure, known zero-submission timeout, possible-submission timeout and delayed completion. Definite failure records 40 integer fixture units and returns undelivered allowance; unknown outcomes retain 100 fixture units until settled. Canceling an uncertain attempt preserves the hold; a late known result becomes paid history without current selection. An old fence cannot select or release newer ownership.

Three worker processes exited while their two-member media groups survived. Wrong process-start identity refused termination; lease uncertainty retained the render lock. SIGTERM followed by bounded SIGKILL escalation confirmed every recorded member stopped before releasing the lock and allowing replacement work. No heartbeat-only inference.

**Failures retained:** [first run](S3-first-run.json) exposed missing cancellation persistence for `recovery_required` in the disposable Store. The accepted policy already permits cancellation with continued liability/lock until cessation; the witness was corrected to persist that intent, not to release liability early. All S3 cases were rerun. A sandbox process-inspection denial interrupted a diagnostic run; successful runs used explicitly approved local access. Child stdio was detached and identity startup bounded to avoid inheriting a parent capture pipe indefinitely.

**Limitations:** authoritative fake receipts model capabilities, not a real provider guarantee. Retrieval, cancellation, billability, billing delay and hidden SDK retries remain S4. Local boot/start identity and process-group stop are demonstrated, not target-host service configuration; Linux deployment must use sufficiently precise process-start identity and group supervision. Metadata/financial registration is one transaction; filesystem durability is separate. Host/power/controller failure is not simulated.

**Consequence:** retain one controlled Python worker with persisted attempts, fencing and blocked uncertainty. Production must implement confirmed cessation/reconciliation; an expired lease is never permission for overlapping paid/media work. No architecture redesign was required.
